3.14 Studio

For HEC Paris students. Learn the Odyssey 3.14 approach, then apply it to a real business: map the dominant model, surface its orthodoxies, explore the 14 directions and stress-test your ideas. A Socratic AI coach reviews each step without doing the work for you.

Three pillars

Select a pillar on the wheel or below to explore it.

1. Value proposition

What you offer, to whom, and at what price.

  • Customers and non-customers
  • Offer: product, service, experience
  • Price and how customers pay

2. Value architecture

How you create and deliver that value.

  • Value chain activities
  • Resources and competencies
  • Partners and ecosystem

3. Contributions

What the model gives back.

  • Financial: revenue, costs, capital employed
  • Social: people and communities
  • Environmental: the planet

Why "3.14"? Three pillars to describe a business model, fourteen directions to reinvent it. Describe the model as it is today, then as it could be tomorrow.

Three critical prerequisites

1. Anticipating trends

Spot the shifts that will reshape your industry before your competitors do.

Tesla bet on electric cars while diesel still dominated.

2. Building on customer insights

Watch how people really buy and use the offer, and design for that.

Decathlon designs its B'Twin bikes around how different people actually ride.

3. Challenging industry beliefs

Question the rules that everyone in the industry takes for granted.

Cirque du Soleil: a circus without animals. Tesla: cars without petrol. Grameen Bank: lending to the poor.

Three stages

1. Understand the dominant model

Describe the industry's model pillar by pillar. Then list its orthodoxies: the unwritten rules everyone takes for granted.

2. Generate ideas

Challenge each orthodoxy with the 14 directions. Aim for quantity first, then combine ideas into coherent alternative models.

3. Test and implement

Check the contributions, list the riskiest assumptions, run cheap experiments, and plan how the new model coexists with the old one.

Combine the directions

Strong business models rarely rely on one direction alone. Castalie reaches the non-customers of bottled water and cuts their costs at the same time; Fairphone reworks several steps of the phone value chain. Use the 14 directions as a checklist, then combine the best ideas into one coherent model.

An orthodoxy is a belief everyone in the industry shares without questioning it: "customers must…", "we always…", "you can't make money unless…". Write each one as a rule. Look at all three pillars. Challenging industry beliefs is one of the three prerequisites of the approach.
